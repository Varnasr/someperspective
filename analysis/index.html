Comparative

  • India is now the worst-ranked of its peer democracies on press freedom

    The relevant comparison set for Indian press freedom is now Turkey, not Brazil or South Africa.

  • A regime comparison, computed live from the data

    The regime comparison is mechanical — averaging, not interpretation. It does not flatter either side.

  • India and Hungary are now travelling the same road

    A democracy can fall this far while remaining electorally competitive — Hungary showed it first.

  • South Africa is the only peer that recovered on press freedom

    Decline isn't inevitable — peer countries have demonstrably reversed it.

Correlation

  • Press freedom and statistical suppression move in lockstep

    The decline in two ostensibly separate institutions is empirically a single phenomenon.

Democracy

  • India ranks 157th of 180 on press freedom

    India's press environment is now judged worse than that of every immediate neighbour except Afghanistan.

  • India's V-Dem score has fallen furthest in absolute terms

    On the most-cited cross-country democracy index, India has erased four decades of liberalisation in one.

  • The Democratic Quality Index is the lowest it has been

    No single index tells the whole story, but every credible index points the same direction.

  • Opposition incarceration cases were near-zero — until they weren't

    A specific tool of political pressure shifted from rare to routine within a single decade.

  • V-Dem score is at 1976-emergency levels

    A democracy can be functionally undone without ever being formally suspended.

Federalism

  • Fiscal centralisation is at its highest in the modern era

    GST plus cesses mean states retain less of the divisible pool than at any point since the 14th Finance Commission framework.

  • Central cess and surcharge share of tax revenue tripled

    A nominally federal tax structure has been redirected by changing the labels on the revenue itself.

Growth

  • Rural household income has been flat for twelve years

    Despite GDP up 90% cumulatively, the rural household is no better off in real terms than in 2014.

  • The 2020 GDP contraction was the steepest since independence

    The recovery was reported in GDP terms but not lived in employment terms.

  • GDP growth volatility has risen — the boom and bust are both larger

    Average GDP growth is a useful number — but for India today, the spread around the average is the story.

Growth vs jobs

  • Growth and formal jobs have moved in opposite directions for a decade

    Headline GDP growth and labour market quality are decoupled — not just unrelated, but opposed.

Indices

  • All three constructed indices point the same direction

    The three indices are correlated because the underlying processes are correlated — not by construction.

Inequality

  • The top 1% income share is at colonial-era levels

    A growing economy has redistributed upward for twelve straight years.

  • The top 1% holds 40% of the country's wealth

    Within India's already-high inequality, wealth inequality is meaningfully higher than income inequality.

  • The bottom 50% income share has been falling since 2018

    A literal majority of Indians has a smaller slice of national income today than they had in 2014.

Jobs

  • Graduate unemployment is five times the headline rate

    Aggregate labour data masks the structural mismatch facing the educated workforce.

  • Women's labour force participation has fallen since 2014

    Indian women's formal participation in the labour market regressed during a growth boom.

  • Gig and platform workers grew eleven-fold in twelve years

    The jobs that replaced shrinking formal employment carry none of formal employment's protections.

  • Informal employment share crossed 89%

    Nine in ten Indian workers have no statutory pension, EPF, ESI, or written contract.

  • Youth unemployment peaked at 18% during COVID

    The cohort that should be powering growth is the one most excluded from formal work.

  • The post-2017 formal employment drop is visible in the data

    A policy regime can be visible in a labour market series even before the headline reports catch up.

  • Twelve consecutive years of formal employment decline

    This is the most-consistent multi-year trend in the entire labour-market dataset.

Methodology

  • What goes into the Statistical Suppression Index

    The index is transparent enough that anyone can replicate it from the public record of statistical events.

  • Why the Democratic Quality Index uses a geometric mean

    Democratic quality is multiplicative, not additive — one weak pillar undermines the rest.

  • What goes into the Fiscal Centralisation Index

    The FCI is a snapshot of how much fiscal autonomy states have lost in measurable, audited terms.

Public services

  • Open Defecation Free coverage is a textbook logistic curve

    Where there is sustained political will and clear measurement, India's state can move very fast.

Society

  • Reported communal incidents are 40% above their 2004 baseline

    Even with conservative counting, the floor of communal violence has risen substantially.

Statistics

  • Statistical suppression spiked to a peak, then stayed high

    A democratic state is being audited with less independent data than before — the acute events eased, but the institutional damage is permanent.

  • Exam disruption events have multiplied

    The institutions that gate the labour market are showing visible strain.

  • The Census is 15 years overdue and counting

    India is the world's most populous country and has not formally counted itself in 15 years.

  • India's 5.1% unemployment rate is unusually low for the income level

    The headline unemployment rate is a misleading benchmark for India because of the definition of 'employed'.
