37 readings of the data

Each entry takes one relationship in the dataset and states what it shows and what follows from it. These are standing analyses and carry no date. They are rebuilt from the dataset on every refresh, so they always reflect the current figures. Data vintage: 2026-10-10.

Comparative

  • India and Turkey rank lowest of the peer democracies on press freedom

    On press freedom India now ranks close to Turkey and far below Brazil and South Africa, both of which improved over the period.

  • A regime comparison, computed live from the data

    The regime comparison is mechanical. It averages the data and interprets nothing, and it does not flatter either side.

  • India and Hungary are now travelling the same road

    Hungary's score fell by a similar amount over the same years, and both countries continued to hold competitive elections.

  • Brazil and South Africa improved on press freedom while India fell

    Press-freedom ranks can recover within a decade. Brazil rose 59 places between 2014 and 2026.

Correlation

  • Press freedom and statistical suppression worsened together after 2014

    The two series move in the same direction. The correlation is moderate and does not by itself show a common cause.

Democracy

  • India ranks 157th of 180 on press freedom

    India ranks below Pakistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal, Sri Lanka and the Maldives. Of its neighbours, only Myanmar (166), Afghanistan (175) and China (178) rank lower in 2026.

  • India's V-Dem score has fallen furthest of its five-country peer group

    India's 2025 score of 0.260 is its lowest on V-Dem's series since 1950, and slightly below the 0.265 recorded in 1976, during the Emergency.

  • The Democratic Quality Index is the lowest it has been

    No single index tells the whole story, and every credible index points the same direction.

  • Central agencies arrested senior opposition leaders in 2023 and 2024

    The arrest of a sitting chief minister in 2024 had no precedent. A yearly count would need a published case list, which does not yet exist.

  • V-Dem score is at 1976-emergency levels

    India's V-Dem score fell to Emergency-era levels without any formal suspension of civil liberties.

Federalism

  • Fiscal centralisation peaked in 2020 and has fallen since

    The NDA's higher average rests on GST and the 2020 borrowing conditions. On the four measured fiscal ratios alone the two eras are close, 0.49 under UPA and 0.45 under NDA.

  • Cesses and surcharges reached a quarter of Union tax revenue in 2020-21

    Revenue raised as a cess stays with the Union, so the cess share measures how much of the tax take sits outside the pool that states share in.

Growth

  • Rural household income rose 27% in real terms between 2016-17 and 2021-22

    Rural incomes grew in real terms between the two surveys, and rural debt grew alongside them.

  • The 2020 GDP contraction was the steepest since independence

    The recovery showed up in GDP and did not show up in employment.

  • GDP growth swung more after 2014, and the pandemic accounts for the difference

    The pandemic year drives the wider spread. Outside it, growth after 2014 was no more volatile than before.

Growth vs jobs

  • Formal job coverage fell while the economy grew

    Headline GDP growth did not bring a larger share of workers into jobs with social security.

Indices

  • All three constructed indices point the same direction

    On era averages all three indices are worse under NDA. The FCI gap is the smallest of the three and rests on its two coded components.

Inequality

  • The top 1% income share is at its highest since 1922

    Over two decades the top 1% took a larger share of national income. The bottom half lost ground until 2017 and has recovered part of it since.

  • The top 1% holds 40% of the country's wealth

    Within India's already-high inequality, wealth inequality is meaningfully higher than income inequality.

  • The bottom 50% income share fell until 2017 and has partly recovered

    The bottom half of India held a smaller share of national income in 2022-23 than in 2004, and a slightly larger one than in 2014.

Jobs

  • Graduate unemployment is four times the overall rate

    Aggregate labour data masks the structural mismatch facing the educated workforce.

  • Women's labour force participation has risen sharply since 2017-18

    The headline rate does not show what kind of work the rise is made of.

  • Gig and platform workers nearly tripled between 2011-12 and 2019-20

    The jobs that replaced shrinking formal employment carry none of formal employment's protections.

  • Nine in ten Indian workers have no social-security cover

    Roughly nine in ten Indian workers have no social-security cover, a larger share than in 2017-18.

  • Youth unemployment has fallen from 17.8% to 9.9% since 2017-18

    The cohort that should be powering growth is the one most excluded from formal work.

  • Formal job coverage dipped in the pandemic year and has only partly recovered

    The share has not returned to its 2017-18 level in any later year of the survey.

  • Formal job coverage fell by 1.4 points over six years of the labour survey

    More than half of India's regular salaried employees have no social security, a larger share than in 2017-18.

Methodology

  • What goes into the Statistical Suppression Index

    The index is transparent enough that anyone can replicate it from the public record of statistical events.

  • Why the Democratic Quality Index uses a geometric mean

    Democratic quality is multiplicative. One weak pillar undermines the rest.

  • What goes into the Fiscal Centralisation Index

    The four measured ratios come from audited public accounts. The two coded components are judgements, and the method page sets them out.

Public services

  • Rural toilet access rose fastest under Swachh Bharat

    Rural toilet access rose more between 2014 and 2019 than in the previous decade, on both programme and survey figures.

Society

  • The Home Ministry's count of communal incidents stops in 2017

    On the ministry's own count, communal incidents in 2014-2017 were close to the UPA-decade level. The record after 2017 is missing.

Statistics

  • Statistical suppression spiked to a peak and has eased since

    The acute events have eased and the census is back on a schedule. The shelved GDP back-series and the NSSO-NSO merger still count at full weight.

  • Exam leaks prompted a national law in 2024

    The 2024 law and the cancellations show that leaks reached national examinations. Measuring how often they happen would need a published list of events.

  • India has not counted its population since 2011

    By the time the 2027 enumeration is complete, sixteen years will have passed between counts.

  • India's unemployment rate is close to other lower-middle-income countries'

    The headline unemployment rate is a misleading benchmark for India because of the definition of 'employed'.

  • GDP counts the rent on your flat and nothing for the cooking

    The production boundary is a convention. India collects the hours needed to price what falls outside it and does not do so.
