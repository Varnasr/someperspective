What changed, and when

Updates

A plain-language record of what changed on this site and when. Data on this site is revised as official releases land, and corrections are made in public rather than quietly — so this page exists to make both visible. Every entry maps to a tagged release; the full technical detail, including internal refactors not worth listing here, is in the repository's CHANGELOG.

2026-08-15  ·  v2.39.1

Readable type on a phone

Nearly all the text on the site was rendering at caption size on a phone. It now reads at normal size.

  • Fixed — Measured on a 390-pixel screen, 94.5% of all visible text on the front page was rendering at 12 or 14 pixels, and only 0.4% at 16. An earlier accessibility pass had raised a 9-pixel floor to 12, which stopped text being unreadable but left the whole page at the size normally used for captions. The scale now moves up a step on phones: body text reads at 16 pixels, paragraphs in the smaller style at 15, and secondary labels at 13.5. Chart labels, table cells and the tab bar are deliberately left where they were, because their size holds the layout together. After the change, 88% of visible text is 15 pixels or larger and no paragraph anywhere renders below 15.
  • Fixed — On a 320-pixel screen — the narrowest phone still in common use — the header row was 24 pixels wider than the display, which pushes the whole page sideways. It now wraps at that width. Checked at nine screen widths from 320 to 1440 pixels across all fourteen sections: no horizontal overflow anywhere.
  • Fixed — The stylesheet was requested without a version stamp, so a returning visitor could pair newly published markup with a stylesheet up to ten minutes old. It is now stamped with the site version, and a build check fails if the two ever drift apart. The guided walkthrough's data requests are stamped the same way, so the deck cannot draw yesterday's numbers from a cache.
  • Removed — The shareable PNG of the three indices, added in 2.37.0 for a social-media launch, has been withdrawn along with the post text. The chart itself remains on the site, where it is drawn live from the dataset and can be exported at any time from the Three Indices section.
2026-08-15  ·  v2.39.0

A declared interest, corrected V-Dem inputs, a loader that recovers, and a sitewide audit

The author's political affiliation is now stated on the site rather than denied. Three errors in this project's own figures are corrected, including a headline growth rate that had been quoted eight ways. And a full audit of every page, on desktop and phone, fixed what it found.

  • Correction — The footer previously said 'No political affiliations.' That was false. The author is a Manmohan Singh Fellow of the All India Professionals' Congress, a department of the Indian National Congress, and this project compares a Congress-led government with its successor. The claim is replaced by a declaration of interests, stated on the site, in the media kit, in the guided walkthrough and in the paper's Declarations. The research remains unfunded and no party body commissioned, reviewed or approved it — but that is a separate fact from neutrality, and the site no longer conflates the two.
  • Correction — Two of the Democratic Quality Index's four components are V-Dem series. The table computing the index held approximated rather than published values for both, and the approximations were too high in the UPA decade — a Liberal Democracy value of 0.555 for 2014 against a published 0.488, and a civil-society value of 0.87 against a published 0.669. Because the error inflated the earlier baseline and left the later years roughly right, it exaggerated the measured decline. It ran in this project's own favour. Both columns are now the published V-Dem series verbatim. The UPA-decade DQI average falls from 0.59 to 0.57 and the 2014 value from 0.54 to 0.49; 2026 is unchanged at 0.29, and the eight-of-nine cross-era result is unaffected.
  • Correction — The international comparison had its 2014 column on V-Dem's Electoral Democracy Index and its 2026 column on the Liberal Democracy Index — two different instruments read as one series. India's decline was shown as 0.71 → 0.26; the like-for-like figure on the Liberal Democracy Index is 0.488 → 0.260. All five countries are now on the same index throughout. India's proportional fall drops from 63% to 47%, and Turkey's is now the steeper of the two; India retains the largest absolute fall in the cohort. The multi-country chart was also labelled 'DQI', which was wrong: the DQI is constructed for India alone and cannot be computed for the others. It is now labelled as what it plots.
  • Added — A build check that fails unless the international table and the index's component table report the same V-Dem figure for India — which they can only do if both are on the same V-Dem index. The earlier consistency check could not have caught this, because it compares published documents against the dataset and both figures were in the dataset.
  • Fixed — The page was starting itself twice. Alpine calls a component's init automatically, and an extra x-init called it again: two requests for the dataset, two sets of event listeners, two chart initialisations. Because the 'live data unavailable' flag latched once set, a single dropped request out of the two was enough to raise that warning on a page whose data had in fact loaded.
  • Fixed — The dataset request now retries three times with backoff, and the warning banner carries a working retry button that clears itself if the retry succeeds. Previously one failed request left the banner up for the rest of the session with no way back.
  • Fixed — The site's main page is a single large file. If the transfer was cut part-way through — a dropped mobile connection, an intermediary that truncates — the page came up looking almost normal but with computed figures silently blank: an empty scorecard, a sentence reading 'On [nothing] cross-era measures'. There is now a check that detects a page which failed to start and says so, with a reload button, instead of leaving blanks.
  • Fixed — Index figures quoted in prose had drifted apart between sections: the SSI appeared as both 8.2 and 6.4, the FCI as both 0.68 and 0.80, the DQI as 0.40 in one place and 0.29 in another, and graduate unemployment as 27% and 28.5% against a dataset value of 26.5%. Every one of these is now read from the dataset at page load rather than typed into the page, so they cannot drift again.
  • Fixed — The animated timeline opened on 2014, where the SSI is 0.0 — so the first thing the panel showed was 'SSI Score: 0', which reads as a broken figure rather than a result. It now opens on the latest year, states which year it is showing, and explains what a zero SSI means: the index counts documented suppression episodes, and none had begun by 2014.
  • Correction — The headline growth figure was wrong. 'India's GDP grew at 6.8% annually since 2014' appeared in eight places across the site and the documents, and it is not what the published series says on any basis. The arithmetic mean for 2014-2026 is 6.1%, which is what the research paper has always reported — so the site's front page and its own paper disagreed. This error ran in the government's favour rather than this project's, but it is an error either way. Every instance now reads 6.1%, with the ex-pandemic figure of 7.2% given alongside it so the 2020 contraction is not quietly doing the work in either direction. The UPA comparison figure is corrected from 7.7% to 7.1% on the same basis.
  • Correction — The executive summary quoted India's press-freedom rank of 161 and labelled it 2024. 161 is the 2023 figure; 2024 was 159. The metric panel now carries the latest year throughout — 157 for 2026 — and the consistency check has been widened to catch value-before-year phrasing, which is the ordering that had been hiding this one.
  • Changed — The same work was being cited five different ways across the site, with two different titles, two different years and a period of 2014-2025 that stopped a year before the data does. Everything now cites one form: Raman, V.S. (2026), Some Perspective: India's Political Economy, 2004-2026. The research paper keeps its own distinct title, as it should.
  • Fixed — A sitewide audit ran every page at desktop and phone width, checking for script errors, failed requests, broken links, duplicate identifiers, sideways scrolling, skipped heading levels, images without alternative text and touch targets below the minimum size. It found the methodology page scrolling sideways on a phone (a long formula and an unbreakable URL), contents links and tags too small to tap reliably, and heading levels skipping from h2 to h4 in four documents. All are fixed. Ninety-eight of a hundred page-and-screen combinations now come back completely clean; the two remaining are elements that are empty or hidden by design.
  • Fixed — Seven of the downloadable documents had no description, no canonical URL and no social-preview tags, so sharing a link to any of them produced a bare URL with no title or image. All seven now carry them.
  • Fixed — The retired research presentation declared the walkthrough as its canonical address while actually redirecting readers to the paper. It now points both at the paper.
2026-08-15  ·  v2.37.0

Correcting how we read the inflation result

The one measure that improved was being described too generously. It is not evidence of good policy, and the paper now says so.

  • Correction — Earlier wording called the fall in inflation 'the single most defensible economic achievement of the period' and attributed it to an institution being strengthened. That reads as crediting the government, and it does not survive scrutiny. The comparison straddles a global commodity cycle: Brent crude fell from about $115 a barrel in June 2014 to under $30 by January 2016 — around 75% — driven by US shale supply and OPEC defending market share, in an economy importing roughly 86% of its crude. A shock that size, arriving exactly at the boundary between the two periods, explains more of the gap than any change of government.
  • Changed — Where domestic policy did contribute, the mechanism was inflation targeting — a rule that constrains the executive's discretion. If that is the channel, it supports the project's argument rather than qualifying it. And the 2026 figure sits on the reweighted CPI basket, so it prints lower for the same price movements.
  • Added — A shareable chart of the three indices, 2004-2026, is now available as a PNG.
2026-08-15  ·  v2.36.0

Accessibility: touch targets and readable text on phones

Buttons, links and controls that were too small to tap reliably on a phone, and text as small as 9px, are fixed.

  • Fixed — Around forty interactive elements were smaller than the 44-pixel minimum on a phone — chart download buttons and theme toggles at 28 pixels, footer links only 14 pixels tall, filter chips at 27, and the trajectory sliders with a touch area just 4 pixels high. All now meet the minimum on touch devices.
  • Fixed — Text rendering as small as 9px on phones is raised to a 12px floor. No text on the site now renders below 12px on a phone.
  • Changed — These changes apply only to touch devices and narrow screens. The desktop layout keeps its existing density — a mouse is precise, a fingertip is about ten millimetres.
  • Note — Links inside a sentence and the tap-to-explain glossary terms are deliberately left at text size. Enlarging them would break the line spacing of every paragraph containing one, and the accessibility guidelines specifically exempt targets positioned by the flow of text.
2026-08-15  ·  v2.35.0

The theory chapter moves into the paper; two presentations retired

Original theoretical work that only existed inside a slide deck is now part of the research paper, and the two hand-maintained presentations have been withdrawn.

  • Added — The paper gains a section setting out where this work sits in the literature and what it adds: a refinement of modernisation theory (growth can legitimise erosion, not only produce democracy), an extension of Levitsky and Way's competitive authoritarianism toward incremental and legal erosion, and a proposed concept — 'fiscal authoritarianism', the use of federal fiscal architecture to centralise power while leaving the constitution formally intact. It also gains a research agenda: state-level variation, sectoral and social disaggregation, district-level indices, and comparative work.
  • Changed — The two presentation documents have been retired. Their content is now either in the paper or in the guided walkthrough, both of which stay in step with the dataset. The old links still work and redirect rather than breaking.
  • Changed — The paper's policy section now carries the fuller set of immediate and structural reforms, stated as directions the measurements point in rather than as costed proposals.

A guided walkthrough, and a responsive audit

2026-08-15  ·  v2.33.0

The research paper, and a correction to a headline inequality figure

The full working paper is published, and an error in our own summary documents — one that ran in this project's favour — is corrected in the open.

  • Added — 'Growth Without Accountability: Measuring India's Institutional and Distributional Transformation, 2004-2026' — a 15,000-word working paper, available to read online or as a 43-page PDF. It documents the measurement problem, specifies how the three indices are built, reports the results, tests them against an independent study, and states the limitations at length.
  • Correction — Four of this project's summary documents stated the 2014 top 1% income share as 15%, which made inequality appear to rise by 7.6 percentage points to 2023. That figure is wrong: 15.0% is the 2014 bottom 50% share, and the two series had been crossed. The World Inequality Database series this project follows has 21.3%, making the rise 1.3 points. The documents are corrected and the framing rewritten around the two facts that are both true and stronger — the top 1% share is at its highest level since 1922, and the bottom half of the population lost roughly an eighth of its share of national income in twelve years.
  • Changed — An automated check now compares every year-labelled figure in the published documents against the source dataset on each change, so a contradiction of this kind cannot pass silently again.
  • Added — The research package now delivers what it advertised: a complete ZIP with every document, the paper, the dataset and the replication code, plus CSV and Excel exports.
2026-08-15  ·  v2.31.0

An independent check, August data, and three rendering fixes

Added an external study that reaches this project's governance conclusion by a different method, refreshed the data to August 2026, and fixed three display bugs — one of which had been hiding a chart's data entirely.

  • Added — An independent-corroboration section in Methodology covering Grier & Grier (2026), 'Promises, Promises'. They build a synthetic-control counterfactual over 1984-2013; this project builds composite indices from observed series. Different method, convergent conclusion — with the points of disagreement stated openly rather than smoothed over.
  • Data — CPI 2026 updated to 4.45% (MoSPI July 2026). Unemployment re-cited to the June 2026 PLFS bulletin — the headline 5.5% was unchanged.
  • Correction — MoSPI replaced the CPI 2012=100 index with 2024=100 from the January 2026 print, reweighting on HCES 2023-24. Food's weight fell from 45.86% to 36.75%, so headline CPI now responds less to a food-price shock than it used to. The 2026 figure is not on the same basis as 2014-2025 and is now labelled as such rather than silently spliced onto the older series.
  • Fixed — The Era Comparison detail chart had been rendering with no data line at all — a colour rule matched none of its points and made the whole series transparent. It now draws correctly, split into NDA-I and NDA-II.
  • Fixed — On phones the whole site was zooming out because one wide element stretched the page to roughly three times the viewport width. All fourteen tabs now fit a phone screen properly.
  • Fixed — Era shading and policy-event markers on the Interactive Data chart were never drawing.
  • Security — The two third-party scripts the site loads are now pinned with integrity hashes, so a substituted file on the CDN cannot execute.

2026-06-18  ·  v2.28.0

Indices rebuilt: COVID undercount, NSO merger, GST

The three constructed indices were rebuilt from a single canonical script so both political eras are computed identically.

  • Changed — The Statistical Suppression Index now includes the COVID mortality undercount and the NSO merger; the Fiscal Centralisation Index now treats GST as a structural shift in state taxing power rather than only counting cesses.
  • Method — Both the UPA and NDA decades are now generated by the same script, so no difference between the eras can come from how they were computed.
