Work

Unemployment, participation and the kind of work people do. Most of these series begin in 2017-18 with the Periodic Labour Force Survey, so they have no earlier counterpart on the same basis.

Every chart runs from 2004 at the left edge to 2026 at the right and changes side between 2013 and 2014. Dashed lines are the two period averages. A gap is a year with no published figure.

Unemployment rate, ages 15 and over

2014-2026 average4.3%
Latest, 20265.2%

Published from 2015. There is no earlier figure on the same basis, so the two periods cannot be compared on this series.

Source and method

2015: Labour Bureau, Fifth Annual Employment-Unemployment Survey (2015-16), usual principal status, 5.0. 2014 and 2016 are empty: the Labour Bureau published no survey for 2014-15 or 2016-17, and its 2013-14 survey (4.9) belongs to an earlier year. The Labour Bureau series is a different survey from PLFS. 2017-2025: annual rates on usual status (ps+ss). From 2017 a year is the PLFS survey year that starts in July of it (2017 = 2017-18), through 2023 (2023-24); 2024 and 2025 are MoSPI's calendar-year rates. 2026: the average of the 8 PLFS Monthly Bulletins published for 2026 (January to August 2026: January 5, February 4.9, March 5.1, April 5.2, May 5.5, June 5.5, July 5.1, August 5), all-India, persons 15+, rural + urban. BASIS BREAK: the monthly bulletins report current weekly status (CWS), which counts a person as unemployed on the past week's activity and runs well above usual status. The 2026 value is therefore a different measure from the years before it, and most of the rise from 3.1 per cent in 2025 is the change of measure. On the same weekly measure MoSPI's calendar-year rate for 2025 was 5.3 per cent.

Workers in regular jobs with social security

2014-2026 average10.4%
Latest, 202310.1%

Published from 2017. There is no earlier figure on the same basis, so the two periods cannot be compared on this series.

Source and method

Derived from MoSPI PLFS published tables (api.mospi.gov.in, retrieved 10 October 2026), usual status (ps+ss), all-India, persons: the share of regular wage/salaried workers among all workers, multiplied by the share of regular wage/salaried employees with any social security benefit (100 minus the published share without any). 2017-18: 22.8% x 50.4% = 11.5; 2018-19: 23.8 x 48.1 = 11.4; 2019-20: 22.9 x 45.8 = 10.5; 2020-21: 21.1 x 46.2 = 9.7; 2021-22: 21.5 x 47.0 = 10.1; 2022-23: 20.9 x 46.1 = 9.6; 2023-24: 21.7 x 46.6 = 10.1. The social security table covers regular wage/salaried employees in the non-agricultural sector; regular wage work in agriculture is about 1% of workers. Years are PLFS survey years starting in July of the year shown. 2014-2016 (before PLFS) and 2024 onward (no annual table yet) are left empty.

Gig and platform workers, millions

2014-2026 average5.8
Latest, 20196.8

Published from 2017. There is no earlier figure on the same basis, so the two periods cannot be compared on this series.

Hollow markers are published estimates or projections, not outturns, and no average uses them: 2020, 7.7, NITI Aayog projection for 2020-21 (Table 14), which the Ministry of Labour cites as the current estimate; 2025, 14.3, NITI Aayog projection for 2025-26 (Table 14).

Source and method

NITI Aayog, India's Booming Gig and Platform Economy (June 2022), Table 14, gig workers in millions, usual principal and subsidiary status. 2017-2019 are NITI's actual estimates for the PLFS survey years starting in July of the year shown (2017-18 5.3, 2018-19 5.4, 2019-20 6.8). NITI's table labels 2020-21 (7.7 million) a projection, though the Ministry of Labour cites it as the current estimate, so it is held in the estimates block with the 2025-26 projection (14.3 million) and drawn as hollow markers. 23.5 million for 2029-30 is outside the site's range. NITI gives no figure for 2014-2016. NITI's 2011-12 estimate is 2.5 million.
