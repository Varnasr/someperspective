What the forecasters expect

Forecasts published by other institutions for India's real GDP growth and CPI inflation. The site does not forecast GDP or inflation itself; its own projections, for series nobody forecasts, are further down. India's fiscal year runs April to March, and every row is on that basis unless its note says otherwise.

MoSPI's Provisional Estimates of 5 June 2026 put real GDP growth for 2025-26 at 7.7 per cent; growth in April to June 2026, the first quarter of 2026-27, was 7.8 per cent (NSO, 31 August 2026). Source.

What each forecast says, in its own words

  • Reserve Bank of India, Monetary Policy Committee. Q1 2027-28 growth projected at 7.1 per cent; the RBI publishes no full-year figure for 2027-28. The publication says: “real GDP growth for 2026-27 is projected at 7.1 per cent ... CPI inflation is projected to be 5.2 per cent for 2026-27”
  • World Bank. Takes 2025-26 as 7.8 per cent, against MoSPI's Provisional Estimate of 7.7. The publication says: “In India, growth is expected to slow to 7.1 percent in FY2026/27, 0.5 percentage points higher than previously expected.”
  • OECD. Inflation is for calendar 2026 (4.2 per cent for 2027). Takes 2025-26 as 7.8 per cent. The publication says: “growth is projected to fall from 7.8% in fiscal year (FY) 2025-26 to 7.1% in FY 2026-27 and 6.5% in FY 2027-28.”
  • Asian Development Bank. ADB calls 2026-27 'FY2026'. The 2027-28 growth figure is from DD News's report of the release (23 September 2026); ADB's page states 4.0 per cent inflation for 2027-28. The publication says: “India's growth is projected at 7.0% in FY2026 (ending 31 March 2027)”
  • IMF. Consumer prices are from Annex Table 1.1.2, also on a fiscal-year basis. Takes 2025-26 as 7.8 per cent, against MoSPI's Provisional Estimate of 7.7. On a calendar-year basis the IMF projects 7.4 per cent for 2026. The publication says: “India 7.8 7.0 7.0 ... For India, data and projections are presented on a fiscal year (FY) basis, with FY 2025/26 (starting in April 2025) shown in the 2025 column.”

The five published forecasts for 2026-27 lie between 7.0 and 7.1 per cent. None of them is a measurement. When a forecast year ends, the site replaces it with MoSPI's published estimate.

What the forecasts do to the comparison

The NDA-period average of real GDP growth is 6.20% over the 12 fiscal years with an outturn, against 6.82% for 2004-05 to 2013-14. Adding 2026-27 at each published forecast gives the averages below. This is arithmetic, with no model.

For the NDA period to match the UPA average, 2026-27 would have to grow 14.3%. No forecaster expects that, so the scorecard row for growth stays where it is whichever forecast comes true. The inflation forecasts are for fiscal 2026-27 and the site's inflation series is by calendar year, so they are not added to its average.

This site's own projections

No institution forecasts these series, so the site projects them itself, to 2026. Three simple models compete: no change from the last value, the last value plus the average yearly change over the previous five years, and a straight line fitted to the previous eight years. Each is scored by a rolling-origin back-test. From every starting year with enough history it forecasts one to four years ahead using only the data available then, and the forecast is compared with what was later published. The model with the smallest average error is used. The interval is the 80th percentile of its back-test errors at that horizon, never narrower than at a shorter horizon; where fewer than three errors exist at a horizon, the one-year error is scaled by the square root of the horizon.

For all three series the model that wins the back-test is the simplest one: no change from the last published value. The trends in these series have reversed often enough that extending them did worse than standing still. The projections therefore say little about direction. What they do give is the range a reader should expect the next published figures to fall in, with an 80% interval built from how wrong the same method would have been in the past.

Projections are drawn as hollow markers with a shaded interval and are never part of an average, an index or the scorecard. The code is tools/build_projections.py and the output data/projections.json.

The Fiscal Centralisation Index is not projected. The 16th Finance Commission fixes the states' share of the divisible pool for 2026-31, but the index also moves with cesses, own revenue and grants, which nobody has fixed, so a projection would be a guess about those.
