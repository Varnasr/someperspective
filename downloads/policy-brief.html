Policy brief for reform

Recommendations for inclusive growth and democratic renewal, drawn from the evidence
May 2026, updated 10 October 2026

Summary

Current policies have produced high GDP growth alongside high inequality, fiscal centralization and democratic erosion. Employment has grown, and the PLFS worker-population ratio rose from 34.7% in 2017-18 to 43.7% in 2023-24. The share of workers in regular jobs with social security fell over the same years, from 11.5% to 10.1%. Without an immediate change of course, these trends risk becoming irreversible by 2030.

The next 18-24 months are the period in which reforms that can restore inclusive growth and strengthen democratic institutions can still be implemented.

Priority reform package in five pillars

The five pillars below are proposals. The budgets and targets attached to them are what the brief proposes. They are not forecasts, because the brief has not modelled how many jobs, how much revenue or how much change in inequality the package would produce.

Pillar 1: Employment emergency response

Immediate actions (0-6 months)

  • National Employment Guarantee Act: Extend MGNREGA to urban areas with 150-day guarantee
  • MSME Revival Package: ₹5 lakh crore credit guarantee scheme at 4% interest
  • Apprenticeship Mission: Target 10 million youth in 12 months
Policy Timeline Budget Target
Urban Employment Guarantee 6 months ₹75,000 cr/year 15M jobs
MSME Credit Scheme 3 months ₹50,000 cr provision 20M jobs protected
Skills & Apprenticeship Immediate ₹25,000 cr 10M trained

Pillar 2: Restoring fiscal federalism

Constitutional reforms required

  • Abolish Cess/Surcharge Loophole: Constitutional amendment to include all taxes in divisible pool
  • Widen the shared pool: The 16th Finance Commission sets the states' share of the divisible pool at 41%, but because cesses and surcharges sit outside it, devolution is 34.7% of gross tax revenue in 2026-27 (budget estimate)
  • GST Council Reform: Voting rights proportional to state GDP contribution
  • Borrowing Autonomy: No reform conditions on state borrowing of the kind attached to the extra borrowing allowed in 2020-21

Cesses and surcharges were 24.4% of the Union's gross tax revenue at their peak in 2020-21, 18.0% in 2024-25 and 14.3% in the 2026-27 budget estimate (15th Finance Commission; Union Receipt Budgets). None of that revenue is shared with the states.

Pillar 3: Reducing inequality

Progressive taxation reforms

Wealth Tax (>₹10 Cr) 0% (wealth tax abolished from 2015-16) 2%
Inheritance Tax (>₹5 Cr) 0% (estate duty abolished in 1985) 25%
Capital Gains Tax 12.5% long-term and 20% short-term on listed equity (since July 2024) 30%
Corporate Tax (>₹100 Cr profit) 22% base, 25.17% with surcharge and cess (section 115BAA) 35%

Universal basic services

  • Healthcare: Increase public spending to 3% of GDP (from 1.9%, Economic Survey 2024-25)
  • Education: Implement Right to Education up to Class XII
  • Nutrition: Universal PDS for bottom 75% population
  • Housing: 50 million affordable homes by 2030

Pillar 4: Independence of the statistical system

Institutional reforms

  • Statistical Commission Act: Grant constitutional status like Election Commission
  • Census 2027: Hold population enumeration on the notified February 2027 schedule, including caste enumeration, and publish results on a fixed timetable
  • Data Transparency Law: Mandatory release of all surveys within 3 months
  • GDP Methodology Audit: Independent international review of 2015 changes
  • Household Production Satellite Account: Publish one, using the Time Use Survey hours already collected

Count the unpaid work

National accounts put a price on the rent of an owner-occupied home and no price on the care given inside it. India runs a Time Use Survey, so the hours are already collected. Women aged 6 and above who do unpaid domestic work spent 289 minutes a day on it in 2024, men 88 (Time Use Survey 2024). Published valuations of that work span 7.5% to 36% of GDP, and the range is that wide because no agency has been asked to narrow it.

  • The ask: a household production satellite account, kept alongside the core accounts, which would remain unchanged. Several statistical systems maintain one.
  • Why it is cheap: the expensive part, the survey, already exists and has run twice.
  • What it would settle: whether the 60% of Indian women outside the counted workforce (PLFS, 2025) are not working, or work that the accounts decline to recognise. 81.5% of women aged 6 and above did unpaid domestic work on the survey reference day (Time Use Survey 2024).
  • Sequencing: a satellite account is a measurement reform and commits no spending. It can precede any decision about what to do with the finding.
Data first: Without reliable data, policy cannot be based on evidence. The suppression of the 2017-18 consumption survey and the delay to the census have left India without basic data, and that must end immediately.

Pillar 5: Strengthening democratic institutions

Immediate measures

  • Electoral Finance Reform: Require parties to disclose the source of every donation. Anonymous cash donations have been capped at ₹2,000 since the Finance Act 2017, and the cap does not reach donations made by other means.
  • Media Independence: Anti-monopoly law limiting ownership to 2 outlets
  • Judicial Autonomy: Collegium appointments within 30 days
  • RTI Strengthening: Reverse 2019 amendments, add penalty clauses
  • Federal Institutions: Restore Planning Commission with state representation

Political economy considerations

Building a reform coalition

  • States: unite on fiscal federalism, which affects every party
  • Business: MSME associations as advocates of reform
  • Civil society: a focus on transparency and employment
  • International: climate finance tied to inclusive growth
  • Youth: the employment guarantee as a cause to organise around

Overcoming resistance

Challenge: vested interests benefit from the status quo.

Strategy: build a countervailing coalition through

  • State government unity across party lines
  • Business case for inclusive growth
  • International pressure on democratic norms
  • Youth mobilization on employment

Costs

The package has not been costed as a whole. The budgets in Pillar 1 are proposals, and the brief makes no estimate of the revenue the tax measures would raise or of their effect on growth. Any such estimate would need a published model behind it.

Cost of inaction

  • Economic: Lost decade of growth, permanent inequality
  • Social: Youth unrest, regional tensions, caste conflicts
  • Political: Democratic breakdown, authoritarian consolidation
  • International: Isolation, lost leadership opportunity

Who should act, and how

Central government: convene an all-party meeting on the economic emergency within 30 days.

State governments: unite on fiscal federalism regardless of party affiliation.

Opposition: make employment and federalism core electoral issues.

Civil society: press for data transparency and democratic accountability.

International partners: link assistance to measures of inclusive growth.
