The story in 90 seconds

GDP grew at an average of 6.2% a year from 2014-15 to 2025-26, against 6.8% in the UPA decade. Excluding the pandemic year 2020-21 the average was 7.3%, higher than the UPA's. The share of workers in regular jobs with social security fell from 11.5% in 2017-18 to 10.1% in 2023-24 (PLFS), and V-Dem reclassified India as an "electoral autocracy" in March 2021. The 2017-18 consumption survey was withheld and the 2021 census postponed. Cesses and surcharges, which states do not share in, reached 24.4% of the Union's gross tax revenue in 2020-21, and the GST took most independent indirect taxation away from the states. In 2022-23, the latest year published, the top 1% took 22.6% of national income, a higher share than at any point since 1922, including the inter-war colonial period (World Inequality Lab). The bottom half's share fell from 18.8% in 2004 to 13.9% in 2017 and was 15.0% in 2022-23.

Declaration of interests

Before you quote this research, you should know who wrote it.

Dr. Varna Sri Raman is a former Manmohan Singh Fellow of the All India Professionals’ Congress (AIPC), a department of the Indian National Congress. This work began before the fellowship. This project compares a Congress-led government (UPA, 2004-14) with its successor (NDA, 2014-26) and concludes against the successor on eight of nine measures. That is a party interest in the conclusion, and it is stated here so no one has to find it out later.

The research is unfunded. No institution, government, party, foundation or commercial entity paid for it. No party body commissioned, reviewed or approved it.

The project makes no claim of neutrality. It offers five things in its place. The complete dataset, the construction code and the source of the paper are published. Both political eras are computed by the same script. The one measure that improved under the current government (inflation) is reported as improved, and the improvement is explicitly not credited to policy. Corrections are published, including one that had run in this project's own favour. And the governance finding is corroborated by an independent study using a different method.

Quotable lines

"By 2022-23 the top 1% received 22.6% of India's national income and held 40.1% of its wealth. The World Inequality Lab puts the income share at its highest since 1922 and above that of South Africa, Brazil and the US, and the wealth share at its highest since its wealth series begins in 1961."
Source: the research findings
"The 2017-18 consumption survey was scrapped in 2019, and the census was postponed in 2020 with no new date until enumeration was notified for February 2027. For most of a decade India had neither a published consumption survey nor a census count newer than 2011."
Source: the statistical analysis section
"An employment elasticity of 0.01 means that a 1% rise in output came with a rise in employment of about 0.01%. The RBI estimates that figure for 2004-05 to 2009-10, and an ORF study estimates it for 2011-16 and puts 2017-23 at 1.11."
Source: Misra and Suresh, RBI Working Paper 06/2014; Ghosh and Roy Bardhan, ORF, 2024
