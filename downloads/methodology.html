1. Methodological overview

This study is descriptive. It sets the record of the UPA decade (2004-2013) beside that of the NDA period (2014-2026) on published statistics, and adds three indices built from documented events and published third-party series. It identifies no causal effects. The method is designed for an economy in which the reliability of official data is itself a research question.

Core principles

  • Every figure is taken from the publisher of that statistic, and the source is named beside it in the data files.
  • A year with no published figure is left empty. Nothing is interpolated or projected into an observed series.
  • Where a figure on the site was found to disagree with its publisher, the site was changed to the published figure, and the correction is recorded in the paper and the technical appendix.
  • All data, the index code and the paper source are public, and the indices can be recomputed with one command.

3. Constructing the indices

3.1 Statistical Suppression Index (SSI)

The SSI is a weighted sum of graded suppression severities (0-10 scale). A persistence (scar) term makes a resolved suppression decay gradually towards zero. It is computed identically for both eras in data/compute_indices.py. The UPA decade records no qualifying event, so its SSI is 0.

SSI_t = Σ(w_i × s_it) where: s_it = severity of stream i in year t ∈ [0,1] (1.0 full suppression/discontinuation; ~0.7 methodology dispute; ~0.3 delay or post-resolution scar) w_i = weight of stream i (see table; max total = 10)

3.2 Fiscal Centralisation Index (FCI)

The FCI measures the shift of fiscal authority from the states to the Union. It averages the components published for each year. Four are published fiscal ratios, each min-max normalised over the years in which it is published; two are coded:

FCI_t = mean of the C_jt published for year t Components: C1: Cess and surcharge share of the Union's gross tax revenue (15th FC Table 3.8, 2011-2018; Union Receipt Budgets, 2019-2026) C2: Tax devolution as % of gross tax revenue, inverted (16th FC Annexure 7.1; Budget at a Glance 2026-27) C3: States' own revenue as % of their revenue receipts, inverted (RBI Handbook 2025-26, Table 94) C4: Non-Finance-Commission grants as % of Union transfers (16th FC Annexure 7.1) C5: Borrowing restrictions, coded (1 in 2020-21, 0.5 in 2021-22) C6: GST structural centralisation (0 pre-2017; full once GST compensation ended on 30 June 2022)

The FCI averages 0.30 under UPA and 0.43 under NDA, peaks at 0.87 in 2020 and is 0.35 in 2026. On the four measured ratios alone the UPA years average 0.49 and the NDA years 0.45, so the higher NDA score comes from the GST and the 2020 borrowing conditions.

3.3 Democratic Quality Index (DQI)

The DQI uses a geometric mean of four published third-party measures to penalize weakness in any dimension:

DQI_t = (V_t × F_t × R_t × C_t)^(1/4) where: V_t = V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index F_t = Freedom House aggregate score / 100 R_t = (180 - RSF press-freedom rank) / 180 C_t = V-Dem Core Civil Society Index

4. Analytical methods

The comparison is between period averages. For each measure the study takes the mean of the UPA years and the mean of the NDA years that have a published figure, and reports which is better. Nine measures are compared on a consistent basis across both periods: GDP growth, CPI inflation, the fiscal deficit, the top 1% and bottom 50% income shares, the press-freedom rank and the three indices. The NDA period is worse on eight of the nine; inflation is the exception.

Series that begin with the Periodic Labour Force Survey in 2017-18 (unemployment on the PLFS basis, youth and graduate unemployment, women's labour force participation, formal employment) have no UPA-period counterpart on the same basis. They are reported for the NDA years and left out of the cross-era comparison.

Employment elasticity is not estimated here. Appendix A5 of the technical appendix reports the published estimates with their sources and periods.

The study runs no difference-in-differences, synthetic-control or structural-break analysis. An earlier version of this page said it did; that was wrong, and the description has been removed.

5. Sources and suppressed data

5.1 One publisher per figure

Each series uses the publisher of that statistic: MoSPI for the labour force surveys, prices and national accounts; the Union Budget documents, the Finance Commission reports and the RBI for fiscal data; the World Inequality Lab for income shares; V-Dem, Freedom House and RSF for the democracy measures. Where two official sources differ, the data file says which one is used and why.

5.2 Dealing with suppressed data

  • The 2017-18 consumption survey was withheld in November 2019. No substitute is constructed, and the years without a published consumption survey stay empty.
  • The census was postponed in 2020 with no date. Houselisting runs from April to September 2026 and population enumeration is notified for February 2027. No population figure is projected here.
  • The new GDP series on base 2022-23 was released on 27 February 2026 without a back-series, so GDP growth before 2023 is on the earlier base.

6. Limitations and caveats

Main limitations

  • There has been no census since 2011, and the next enumeration is notified for February 2027, which limits demographic analysis.
  • The 2017-18 consumption survey was withheld, and no substitute is constructed.
  • National aggregates mask large differences between states.
  • The informal economy remains hard to measure despite improvements in method.
  • The pandemic complicates comparisons involving 2020-2021.
  • Some data sources may face pressure.
  • Every GDP figure used here excludes unpaid household work (see below).

6.1 What GDP does not count

GDP measures work inside a production boundary set by the System of National Accounts. The boundary is a convention. Where a household owns the dwelling it occupies, the accounts estimate the rent it would otherwise have paid and add that to output, because a real service is being consumed. The same reasoning is not applied to cooking a meal in that dwelling, minding a child or nursing a parent. Those services enter the accounts at zero.

India already collects the hours. The NSO ran Time Use Surveys through 2019 and 2024. Among those aged 6 and above who do it at all, women spent 289 minutes a day on unpaid domestic services in 2024 and men 88. Among women aged 15-59 who do it, the figure is 305 minutes. For a woman that is roughly 1,758 hours a year, in no employment statistic and no output series. Published valuations of that work span 7.5% to 36% of GDP. The spread comes from the choice of valuation method, and there is little uncertainty about the hours themselves.

No figure in this assessment is adjusted for it. Every growth rate is the official one on the official boundary. Full treatment: someperspective.info/care and Section 6.11 of the research paper.

7. Robustness

Three checks are reported in the technical appendix (section A7). The SSI is recomputed with every weight moved by 20% either way. The FCI is recomputed without the coded borrowing component, which moves the 2024 value from 0.44 to 0.53. The DQI is recomputed as an arithmetic mean, which moves the 2024 value from 0.28 to 0.33. None of the three changes the direction of the cross-era comparison.

The study reports no bootstrap intervals, Monte Carlo simulations or external-validation correlations. An earlier version of this page listed them, with correlation figures that nothing in the repository computed, and they have been removed.

8. Replication guide

8.1 What the repository holds

Repository: github.com/Varnasr/someperspective

  • data.json: every series shown on the site, with a source note for each.
  • data/compute_indices.py: the SSI, FCI and DQI, their inputs and the sources of those inputs.
  • data/SP_masterdataset.csv: the year-by-year dataset, written from the two files above by tools/build_master.py.
  • replication_code.py and replication_code.R: recompute the cross-era comparison from the dataset.
  • paper/paper.md: the source of the working paper.

8.2 Reproduction steps

  1. Clone the repository: git clone https://github.com/Varnasr/someperspective
  2. Recompute the indices: python3 data/compute_indices.py
  3. Rebuild the dataset: python3 tools/build_master.py
  4. Reproduce the comparison: python3 replication_code.py (standard library only; matplotlib is optional) or Rscript replication_code.R (base R)
