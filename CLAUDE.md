# someperspective

Research site for *Some Perspective: India's Political Economy, 2004-2026*, at
someperspective.info. Static HTML, every page generated by a Python builder
from `data.json` and `data/compute_indices.py`: the front page, the data pages
under `/explore/` (one per topic, plus the states, compare, forecasts, primer
and methods pages), 37 analysis pages, the inflation and care explainers, the
walkthrough and the paper. The downloads are hand-maintained HTML.

## Commands

```bash
# Build order matters: the paper before build_site (the ZIP packages it), and
# build_explore before build_site (the sitemap lists the /explore/ pages).
python3 tools/build_master.py    # data/SP_masterdataset.csv
python3 tools/build_front.py     # index.html
python3 tools/build_paper.py     # downloads/paper.html and the PDF
python3 tools/build_explore.py   # /explore/ pages (and tools/primer_page.py)
python3 tools/build_site.py      # analyses, explainers, walkthrough, sitemap, feed, dataset, ZIP
python3 tools/build_og.py        # link-preview cards, og/<name>.png; run last
python3 replication_code.py      # must reproduce the scorecard
python3 tools/check_palette.py   # palette copies + contrast, run by CI
python3 tools/check_indexability.py
python3 tools/check_data_parity.py
python3 -m http.server 8000      # then open http://localhost:8000
```

## The page is split where the governments changed

Since 2026-10-10 every page shares one design. The page is divided at 10/23 of
its width, the share of the 23 years 2004-2026 that the UPA decade holds: a teal
field on the left (`--field-upa`), a violet field on the right (`--field-nda`)
and a 2px ink line on the seam (`--seam: 43.4783%`). Every chart on the front
page runs from 2004 at the left edge to 2026 at the right, so its 2013/2014
boundary falls on the seam. Change the width of the split and the charts no
longer line up with it. Text sits in slabs: `--paper` panels with a 2px ink
border, no radius, no shadow. One type family, Archivo, with its width axis
(`font-stretch`) doing the work a second face used to.

The tokens kept their old names so that the 48 copies could change in place,
and two of them changed meaning. `--accent` is now ink (controls, kickers,
buttons) and no longer an orange. `--upa` and `--nda` are the two period inks;
use them for anything that says which period a value belongs to, and never the
other way round. `tools/check_palette.py` measures each period ink on its own
field and the text inks on both fields.

`index.html` is generated by `tools/build_front.py`. Do not edit it by hand.

## The data pages replaced the dashboard (2026-10-10)

The old `/explore/` was a 16-tab Alpine.js app with an inline copy of the data.
It is gone. `tools/build_explore.py` writes one page per topic (economy, jobs,
inequality, democracy, statistics, federalism), the states table, a two-series
comparison, the published forecasts, the primer and the sources page, all in the
split design, all read from `data.json` at build time, so there is no second copy
of any figure. Old tab links (`/explore/#economic-trajectory` and the rest) are
forwarded by `LEGACY` in that script.

The four "human stories" are on `/explore/voices/`, from `data/voices.json`.
Three are the author's own fieldwork interviews (confirmed by the author on
2026-10-12); their quotations are verbatim and exempt from the writing check.
Two things from the old app were deliberately not carried over. The methodology tab drew an SSI component chart
and a "data coverage" chart from literals that matched nothing the index
computes. The scenario lab and the 2030 "trajectory" ran on hand-set
coefficients. The trajectory is replaced by `/explore/forecasts/`, which shows
only forecasts other institutions have published (RBI, World Bank, OECD, ADB,
IMF), from `data/forecasts.json`, with the arithmetic of what each GDP forecast
does to the period averages. Below that, the site's own projections for series no
institution forecasts (the two income shares and formal-job coverage), from
`tools/build_projections.py`: three simple models scored by a rolling-origin
back-test, the winner used, and an 80% interval from its back-test errors. All
three series currently pick "no change". Run it after any change to those series,
before `build_explore`; CI runs `--check`. The site does not forecast GDP or
inflation itself: 22 annual points with two base changes cannot beat forecasters
working from monthly and quarterly data.

## Link previews and sharing

`tools/social.py` writes every page's Open Graph and Twitter tags and its share
row (WhatsApp with the text written, X, LinkedIn, email, copy link). Every
builder calls it; the hand-maintained `downloads/*.html` and
`tools/walkthrough_shell.html` carry its output pasted in, so if the tag set
changes, change those too. A page's card is `og/<name>.png`, where the name
comes from its canonical address (`social.og_name`), so a builder and the card
generator agree without reading each other.

`tools/build_og.py` draws the cards from the built pages, so it runs after every
other builder. Each card is the page's own og:title and og:description, and the
first seam chart on the page redrawn across the bottom: a seam chart gives each
year 1/23 of the width, so on a card split at 10/23 the 2013/2014 boundary falls
on the card's seam. `og/manifest.json` holds a hash of what each card was drawn
from, and `--check` (in CI, needs no Pillow) fails on a stale or missing card, a
card with no page, or a page whose og:image points anywhere else. Bump `VERSION`
in the script when the drawing changes. On the walkthrough the share row is
moved into the last slide by the deck script, because the fixed slide
navigation covers the foot of the page.

## Hosting and DNS

The owner's rule for every site (2026-10-12): Porkbun is the registrar and
nothing else; Netlify does everything else. The site is served by Netlify from
this repository (`netlify.toml`): the build runs
`tools/check_publish.py --stage _site`, which copies only the published files,
so the build tools, paper sources and notes here are never served, and a page
linking to something unpublished fails the deploy. The DNS zone is in Netlify
DNS (team Idealog Works, nameservers `dns1-4.p03.nsone.net`) and also carries
Porkbun's two MX records and SPF line for mail forwarding, and the Google
Search Console TXT record. Change a record in Netlify, not at Porkbun.
GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows/static.yml`, `CNAME`) served the site until
2026-10-12 and stays only until the nameserver switch has propagated.

## Estimates are drawn, never averaged

`data.json` has an `estimates` block for published figures that are not
outturns: a Budget Estimate, a NITI projection. `seam_chart(..., est=...)` draws
them as hollow markers joined by a dotted line to the last observed year, and no
average, index or scorecard reads them. `tools/check_data_parity.py` fails if an
estimate sits on a year that also has an outturn. The owner's rule: before
removing an unsourced or projected figure, look for a sourced alternative or a
way to show it honestly. Removing is the last resort.

## The palette is written 48 times

There is no shared stylesheet for the essay and explainer pages: each carries
its own inline `:root` block. That is the arrangement, not something to
refactor here — but it means a colour fixed on one page is fixed on one page.

Before the split redesign, `--accent` was darkened from `#b4530e` to `#a34a0c` on the homepage first, and
the other 46 copies kept the value that fails: **4.44:1 on `--paper` `#f4f1ea`**,
the background of every essay. It passes on white, which is where it had been
checked. `--accent-2` did the same thing at 4.16:1.

`tools/check_palette.py` compares the copies and measures every ink against
every surface in both themes. Three things it has to get right, each established
by getting it wrong:

- **It measures fills as well as inks.** `--accent` is ink on a light page and a
  fill under `--on-accent` on a dark one, and those pull opposite ways.
  Darkening the accent to fix a link makes the button worse. `FILLS` covers that
  direction, and a fault injection confirmed a check without it approves white
  on the dark-theme amber at 2.26:1.
- **Two dark-block shapes.** The essay pages use
  `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`; `index.html` uses a `.dark` class,
  because it has a theme toggle. Reading only the first measures index.html's
  dark theme against its *light* tokens, which is a pass that means nothing.
- **A page with no dark block fails** unless it is exempt with a reason.
  `downloads/research-package.html` is pinned light on purpose, because it is
  written to be printed. A stale exemption fails too.

## What the tokens cannot see

A colour written as a literal in a rule, and white written on a fill, are both
invisible to it. Every one of these was found by running axe over the built
pages in **both schemes** at 1280x900 and 390x844, and none of them would have
shown up in a token check:

- **`dark:text-gray-400` is not the same as `text-gray-400`.** A blanket
  find-and-replace of `text-gray-400` matches inside the `dark:` variant too, so
  52 dark-mode overrides became `dark:text-gray-600`, which is the same value as
  the light one and therefore a no-op. Every muted line on the dashboard
  measured 1.9 to 2.4:1 in dark mode. The same replace ran the other way in the
  footer: `bg-slate-900` is a dark band inside a light page, and
  `text-gray-600` on `#0f172b` is 2.35:1 across fourteen elements.
- **`role="img"` on an ECharts container.** ECharts 6 writes `role="img"` on the
  root when `aria` is enabled, *and* gives its canvas a tabindex for keyboard
  navigation. `role="img"` means "one image, contents not exposed", so the pair
  hides a control a keyboard user can still reach: 28 nodes across the tabs. The
  generated `aria-label` is worth keeping; the role is the error, and
  `getChart()` rewrites it to `figure` after `setOption`.
- **A visible `<label>` above a control associates nothing.** Ten controls on
  the dashboard had one — the three chart selectors, both scatter axes, the era
  indicator and the four scenario sliders — and every one was announced as an
  unnamed field. A `<label>` binds only by wrapping the control or by a matching
  `for`. The glossary search had a `placeholder` and nothing else; a placeholder
  disappears on the first keystroke.
- **Seven walkthrough charts announced as nothing at all.** They were
  `role="img"` with no name. `chartAlt` in `data/walkthrough.json` now describes
  what each one shows; the slide title is the claim rather than the picture, so
  it is only the fallback.
- **Wide tables that no keyboard could reach.** `.table-scroll` scrolls
  horizontally on a phone with no way in from the keyboard, in six files.

## Published data wins

The owner's rule (2026-10-10): where a figure on the site is contradicted by the
published source it names, or by the official publisher of that statistic, the
site changes to the published figure. No permission is needed. A year with no
published figure is left empty (`null`), never filled by interpolation, and the
source note in `data.json` says which years are empty and why. Projections
(NITI's gig workforce after 2020-21, for example) are not observations and do not
go in an observed series.

Checked against the publishers on 2026-10-10, and the series they replaced:
`unemployment` 2017-2020 (MoSPI PLFS), `youthUnemployment` and
`graduateUnemployment` (MoSPI PLFS), `femaleLFPR` (MoSPI PLFS; it had shown a
fall to 21%, PLFS shows a rise to 40%), `gigWorkers` (NITI Aayog 2022, Table 14),
`ruralIncome` (NABARD NAFIS, two rounds only), `formalEmployment` (derived
from PLFS: regular wage share times the share with social security),
`fiscalDeficit` (Union Budget), `top1Share` and `bottom50Share` (World
Inequality Lab WP 2024/09, statistical appendix; the table ends at 2022).
`rupeeUSD` (Federal Reserve H.10 monthly averages), and every input to the
three indices: V-Dem v16, Freedom House, RSF, and the fiscal ratios behind the
FCI (16th and 15th Finance Commission reports, Union Receipt Budgets, Budget at
a Glance, RBI Handbook Table 94). The SSI severities are the author's coding
against the rubric in `data/compute_indices.py`; the events behind them are
dated and sourced.

`data/SP_masterdataset.csv` is written by `tools/build_master.py` from
`data.json` and `data/compute_indices.py`. Do not edit it by hand; CI runs
`--check`, and `replication_code.py` must reproduce the scorecard from it.

MoSPI's PLFS API (`api.mospi.gov.in/api/plfs/getData`) answers with
`frequency_code=1` for annual rates; pass `religion_code=1&social_category_code=1`
or the agricultural-year rows do not come back, and keep `limit` at 100 or less
(a larger limit returns 400). The host needs `OP_LEGACY_SERVER_CONNECT`; see
`tools/refresh_monthly.py`.

## Watch out for

- **MoSPI's data API and MoSPI's press releases disagree on GDP.** On
  2026-10-10 the NAS API returned 7.3, 7.2 and 7.8 for 2023-24 to 2025-26; the
  release of 27 February 2026 gives 7.2 and 7.1, and the Provisional Estimates
  of 5 June 2026 give 7.7 (7.8 is the fourth quarter). The site uses the
  releases. Check any API figure against the release before it goes in.
- **The walkthrough still loads ECharts from jsDelivr.** A local audit of it
  needs the CDN stubbed by route.
- **`data/walkthrough.json` feeds two pages.** `walkthrough/index.html` and
  `tools/walkthrough_shell.html` carry the same renderer; change one and change
  the other.
- **`downloads/` pages each carry their own scheme** and are outside the shared
  palette. They were failing on their own literals: `#059669` as both ink
  (3.6:1) and fill under white (3.76:1), `#9ca3af` at 2.53:1, `#3b82f6` as a
  fill at 3.67:1.

## Testing

`.github/workflows/validate.yml` runs every builder with `--check`, the
publish, writing, indexability, palette, data-parity, docs-consistency and
V-Dem-basis checks, and an internal-link check over every generated page, on
every push and pull request. There is no Node step: Tailwind left with the old
dashboard.

Verified 2026-09-23 with axe-core over all 54 pages in both colour schemes at
390x844 and at 1280x900: **522 serious or critical nodes before, 0 after**, with
no horizontal overflow on any page.
